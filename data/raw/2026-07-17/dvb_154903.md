# DVB-I Book Germany approved, clearing path to market launch

29.05.2026


* * *

The DVB-I Round Table in Germany has approved the “DVB-I Book Germany”, establishing the technical, regulatory and organizational framework for the commercial deployment of DVB-I in the German market. The milestone publication was announced yesterday by the Bavarian Regulatory Authority for New Media (BLM).

The DVB-I Book Germany sets out the principles under which content providers and equipment manufacturers can participate in Germany’s DVB-I ecosystem on an open, non-discriminatory basis. It draws on the [DVB-I Technical Implementation Profile developed by Deutsche TV-Plattform (DTVP), which was published in March 2026](https://dvb.org/news/dvb-i-gathers-momentum-new-zealand-ireland-and-germany-announce-steps-forward/) following a two-year process involving industry stakeholders and international experts.

> “DVB-I offers simple and direct access to commercial and public service TV content via streaming without the need for additional apps,” said Dr Thorsten Schmiege, President of the BLM and Chair of the DVB-I Round Table in Germany. “DVB-I is intended to ensure the availability and findability of traditional broadcast content and linear TV – in a future-oriented way and independent of the commercial interests of individual gatekeepers.”

The Round Table brings together broadcasters, broadcasters’ associations, TV set manufacturers and regulatory authorities, with a market launch targeted for September 2026.

This development comes as DVB-I gathers momentum globally. Freeview New Zealand is [on track to launch Freeview NextGen in Q3 2026](https://dvb.org/news/freeview-nz-to-launch-worlds-first-nationwide-dvb-i-service/) as the world’s first nationwide free-to-air TV platform built on DVB-I, while trials and deployments are advancing in Italy, Ireland, France, Spain and Australia.

> “Built on open standards, the German DVB-I Book defines the comprehensive requirements for service providers and receiver manufacturers ahead of the nationwide launch, marking a major collaborative milestone for all key stakeholders in the German market,” said Remo Vogel, Chair of the DVB Project. “With launch activities progressing in Italy, the recent announcement from New Zealand, and the German market launch now in sight, we are beginning to see the results of this shared international effort.”

The DVB-I Book Germany can be requested at [info@bmt-online.de](mailto:info@bmt-online.de). Further information on DVB-I trials and deployments worldwide is available at [dvb-i.tv/deployments](https://dvb-i.tv/deployments).
